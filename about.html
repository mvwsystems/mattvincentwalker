About
What broke, and what rebuilt me.

I’m Matt. Husband, dad of two, forty-three, just west of Fort Worth. I’m a Christian — not the loud kind. The kind who had to find out what he actually believed at the bottom. This is the story of how I got there and what pulled me out.

My working life never fit one lane. I spent nearly two decades in pastoral ministry — preaching, teaching, and on debate stages as a Christian apologist alongside men like J. Warner Wallace and Josh McDowell. I was also a marketer: the kind who took ten percent of an insurance company’s TV budget, moved it to digital, and broke every inbound record they had. I ran an equipment-rental counter out of college and left a branch manager. When a job disappeared with a baby at home, I sold marketing door-to-door until the money flowed. At a Fortune 500 industrial company, I tripled a territory’s all-time sales record. Formation on one track, leverage on the other. I didn’t know yet that they were the same work.

Then came the years it broke. A territory cut erased a third of my income the same year we closed on the house we’d built. Debt. Creditors. Days at one job, nights at another, drinking to take the edge off, performing “we’re fine” for everyone outside. A marriage that nearly didn’t survive. And one night, at the bottom underneath the bottom, I came closer to not being here than I ever intend to be again.

What pulled me back was my son — who believed I was his hero. I decided to become the man he already thought I was. Not with a breakthrough. With a decision, followed by structure.

I had no motivation left, so I built something that didn’t need any: a minimum daily. Walk through the gym doors, even if I don’t train. Ten sales calls. One note to my wife, every day. Brick by brick, the structure rebuilt the man. Then I went back to school — an M.A. in Executive Coaching from Concordia University Irvine, under Dr. John Townsend — and put everything I’d used to survive, the therapy and the theology and the systems, through one question: how does a man grow under the same weight that should have broken him?

The answer filled twenty years of dotted Moleskine notebooks — I’ve been writing in the same kind since 2006. Sermons. Campaigns. Quotes that wouldn’t let go of me. Questions I couldn’t put down. For most of those years I thought I was collecting notes. It turns out I was building the thing that would eventually save my life. The daily structure became the Vincere OS. The writing became The Vincere Letter — vincere, Latin for “to overcome,” the root of my middle name and the reason I write. Systems came first in my story. AI came second, as the tool that helped me catch up.

Today I coach men — mostly Christian businessmen and fathers who are working themselves into the ground trying to hold their faith, lead their families, and carry a team or a company. And I build the systems and AI inside businesses so the work stops eating the life; that side runs through 6 Signal. I live near Fort Worth with my wife and our two kids. The marriage we fought for gets better every year. I train. I write. I build. I savor my kids differently now. Everything still starts in the notebook.

There’s a book in the notebooks too — Love Your Sin to Death. It’ll take the time it takes.

I work slowly. I take responsibility seriously. And I don’t rush trust.
