The Society: rules that keep it survivable

  • One call a week. Nothing else promised. Ninety minutes. The chat belongs to the members. The hours go to the chat if you let them, so don't.
  • Annual by default. A monthly option priced to push men to annual. Monthly churn is a part-time job you didn't sign up for.
  • Four weeks off a year, announced in advance. Record every call. Build the absence in before it becomes an apology.
  • The Society is the factory. One module a month, taught live. The recordings become the OS. In year two the OS sells self-serve to the man who won't join.
  • Founding members pay less forever. That is the only launch urgency you'll ever need.

The Order: what to guard

  • The price is the dues. The invitation is the filter. Keep them separate. The moment the Order is "whoever can pay," it's a retreat, not an Order.
  • A retreat is an events business. Venue contracts, deposits, insurance, travel. A lodge or a producer handles all of it, or logistics eats more hours than the work.
  • Something in between. A quarterly call and a private thread. A few hours a quarter, and the price holds for the other fifty-one weeks.
  • Four days, not seven. The Americas before an ocean. Your reader has a family. Exotic can wait for year two.
  • It's built last. It can't assemble before coaching has produced alumni. Realistically eighteen months out. That's right: it's the center.

The rules that hold it together

  • Every door gives the Grounding Protocol. One gift, everywhere.
  • Rings one and two run at zero hours. The Society is ninety minutes a week and nothing else promised.
